# English interview guide

The 16 questions reproduce the English wording of the study instrument, with soft line wrapping normalised. They have not been rewritten for this data release. Prompts are the elicitation context; asking a question does not establish that every participant reported a corresponding practice. Other language versions remain in the project archive.

### Q1

We are very glad that you could join this program. If you are comfortable, could you share how you became involved in this Chinese learning project? Also, what kinds of rules or criteria does your country consider when selecting participants to study in China?

### Q2

In your country, what kind of support or attention has been given to this program? For example, has the government made any public statements or organized related celebrations?

### Q3

If you are comfortable sharing, we'd love to hear your personal view: how important was it for you to take part in this program? When you decided to apply for the exam and selection, what were your main considerations or goals? For example, was it more about personal pride, a sense of being entrusted by your country, or were there other motivations you would like to share?

### Q4

Before coming to study in China, had you ever been exposed to or studied Chinese (Mandarin)? If so, would you be willing to share a little about your learning experience?

### Q5

During your studies, have you ever felt anxious or pressured because you were worried about not meeting the requirements or goals set by your country? If so, how did that make you feel at the time — would you be willing to share a bit about your experience?

### Q6

In your view, what factors — whether internal or external — have helped you maintain continuous motivation in learning Chinese?

### Q7

For you personally, do you feel that using English as a medium language has been helpful in learning Chinese? If so, could you explain why?

### Q8

In your opinion, when teachers use 'accommodative language' in class (such as simplifying expressions or slowing down their speech), does this help you communicate with local people outside of class, or does it sometimes create obstacles? Why?

### Q9

Have you taken the HSK Level 3 exam? In your country, is it required to obtain an HSK Level 3 certificate? Why do you think this certificate is needed — is it due to national requirements, your own career development goals, or requirements from the institution where you study Chinese?

### Q10

In your view, which do you consider more important: learning the language or understanding and integrating into the new culture? Do you feel they are equally valuable? Why?

### Q11

Does learning a new culture give you a sense of pride? If so, in what ways does that pride show itself? (For example: identity, professional recognition, approval from family or community, etc.)

### Q12

In your daily life in China, would you be willing to participate in local cultural visits or festival activities organized by your school? If so, how do you usually decide whether to join or not? If you would rather not, we welcome you to share your thoughts or concerns.

### Q13

Please describe your adaptation experiences in daily life in China: From aspects such as weather, food, housing and surroundings, living expenses and payment methods, transportation and public transit, daily routines and noise, medical care and buying medicine, administrative procedures, etc., choose the 2–3 aspects that affected you the most. Explain what happened, how you felt (e.g., surprised, anxious, relaxed, accomplished), and what actions you took to cope (such as seeking help, changing habits, using tools or resources). Briefly say whether these actions were effective and what changes occurred afterwards.

### Q14

Please describe whether you still need to handle work-related tasks from your home country while studying in China. If so, briefly explain what kind of work you do, the main conflicts it creates with your classes or daily schedule (such as time-zone meetings, overlapping schedules, or institutional differences), the most challenging aspect, how you managed it, and what the outcome was.

### Q15

Please describe your experiences interacting with Chinese classmates, teachers, colleagues, or community residents. You may discuss communication styles (direct/indirect, refusal and tact, sense of humor), social habits (inviting/returning invitations, group activities, sense of time, personal relationships and boundaries), relationship building (making and maintaining friendships or collaborations), as well as language barriers and how you addressed them (translation tools, gestures, third-party assistance).

### Q16

During your studies in China, how do you perceive the authority of teachers and the methods of classroom management? For example, attendance taking, leave approvals, assigning holiday homework, after-class check-ins, or assigning tasks. Compared to the educational culture in your home country, do you feel any pressure or discomfort? If so, please describe specific situations, your feelings, and how you understood or coped with them.
